## Source and authority review

- Product Statement is treated as the product's Winning Strategy: Who · What · Which · How. It is held in the Product Brief and carried into every downstream work item through a Strategy Spine: exact version/link and strategic choice, the right target, and observable 100% success. A citation alone is explicitly insufficient. It is not a third peer document or 1–3 sentence communication copy.
- Master Brand Brief and Product Brief remain the two authority levels. A short audience-specific product description is explicitly labelled as a projection derived from the Product Statement and may introduce no new claim.
- The Who · What · Which · How framing follows the current Master Brand Brief and the Brand Owner's clarification of 6 August 2026.
- `[POSITION-01]` in Design System v0.8.8 is visibly identified as carrying the earlier framing and requiring a later source update. The revised copy is not misrepresented as a verbatim extraction from that section.
- Who names the primary user, decision context, functional need and emotional need; buyer, approver and beneficiary are separated when relevant.
- Which separates current workarounds, direct alternatives and aspirational benchmarks that set dimension-specific quality bars.
- How is the evidence-backed way to win within the Which frame.
- The new drafting tips apply only at product level. Company-level Which keeps alternative categories and shared quality dimensions; named competitors belong in a Product Brief or evidence-backed benchmark registry.
- A demanding competitor raises the bar but does not create durable advantage. The candidate labels the compounding mechanism as a testable hypothesis and asks what should accumulate, under which conditions and with what comparative evidence.
- Product comparisons use the same user, task, input and constraints; the same schema / release is required only when the comparison depends on data.
- Competition / quality-bar decisions route to the Product Brief; channels, pricing and funnel decisions route to the GTM plan.
- The location-screening example is labelled as teaching material, not an approved Product Statement.
- The short description trace is Who + What + How + boundary. It is labelled as derived communication copy; Which remains in the governing Product Statement even when the audience-specific sentence does not mention alternatives or benchmarks.

## New-joiner storytelling gate

The first-run sequence borrows the strongest explanatory devices from the Claude reference without adopting its three-layer content model:

1. the first viewport states `Product Statement = Winning Strategy`, rather than the generic phrase “one strategy”;
2. the Strategy Spine immediately requires `Strategy ref · right target · 100% success`, and explains that “carried into every document” means traceability rather than pasting the full statement;
3. the task-first chooser defaults to ordinary downstream work and answers `Open first · Carry forward · Define for this work`;
4. Product Statement then opens into the four strategic choices Who · What · Which · How;
5. one `too vague → Strategy ref → Target → 100% success` worked example separates delivery from outcome proof;
6. a plain-language stop rule and `Strategy · Target · 100% success` ship gate close the route.

The deep drafting guide remains available but closed by default. This keeps emotional need, formidable alternatives, reinforcing choices, evidence, tests and boundaries accessible to strategy authors without making a new joiner read them before completing the five-minute path.

The example states that `100% success` means every agreed, observable condition for calling the work successful. It does not mean the metric itself must equal 100%, and it does not guarantee sales, approval or another downstream outcome outside the work's control. A finished file, production release or KPI may establish `Delivered`; the page requires `Delivered · outcome not yet proven` until the intended user behavior or decision has been tested.

## Gradient surface

The hero and closing use the same governed `measure` recipe in both themes:

`linear-gradient(135deg, #1D4497 0%, #176B82 54%, #08756F 100%)`

The hero deletion comparison is direct: the user identified the flat replacement as a regression, and restoring the measure surface re-established direction / measurement at the entry scene without reducing readability. Foreground contrast was checked against every declared stop:

- white: minimum `5.55:1`;
- cream `#F2F1DF`: minimum `4.87:1`;
- blue tint `#E2E9ED`: minimum `4.52:1`.

The hero overrides the global focus token with a visible `3px` white focus outline because `#176B82` would disappear at the middle gradient stop. Print CSS resets the hero and closing to white surfaces with dark text and a blue count so browsers that omit backgrounds do not print white-on-white copy.
